# The Waiting Tax

**Live:** https://thebullbrew.github.io/waiting-tax/

"I'll buy when the time is right." Fair — but hesitation has a price tag, and nobody hands you the receipt. The Waiting Tax adds up what waiting to buy a home actually costs: rent burned while you wait, price appreciation you absorb, and the heavier monthly payment you lock in later — minus the one thing waiting gives back (what your down payment earns while parked). One honest number, plus the cost per day the meter runs.

## The method

Most rent-vs-buy debates drown in feelings. This one is an invoice with three charges and one credit:

1. **Rent burned** — every month's rent, grown at your expected rent inflation, summed over the wait. Gone forever.
2. **Price appreciation absorbed** — the home gets more expensive while you deliberate; that delta is a cost you chose.
3. **The heavier payment** — a bigger loan (on a pricier house) at whatever rate exists then. Shown as monthly P&I now vs. later, plus extra interest over the first 10 years.

**Minus the credit:** your down payment doesn't sit in a mattress — it compounds at your savings yield while you wait. That's real, so it comes off the bill.

- **The Waiting Tax** — rent burned + appreciation − down-payment growth. The headline number.
- **Cost per day** — the tax divided by every day of the wait. Hesitation, itemized daily.
- **Verdict** — a plain-spoken read: a priced wait is fine when it buys you something concrete (20% down to kill PMI, a cleaner credit score, certainty about the city); it's a donation when the reason is a vibe.
- **Year-by-year meter** — chart and table showing how rent burned and appreciation stack up each year you wait.
- **Rate-shift slider** — model rates falling or climbing while you wait, because "waiting for lower rates" is the most popular fantasy in real estate.
- **Saveable scenarios + copy summary** — park your what-ifs and paste the math into a note, a text, or a spreadsheet.

## How to run

No build, no backend. Open `docs/index.html` in any browser — or install it as a PWA (manifest + service worker included, works offline). Everything is computed client-side; nothing leaves your device.

Estimates only, not financial advice.
